# 2026-09-26 — v0.1: framework, ADM evolution, black-hole data, Ψ₄ extraction

**Branch:** `rkdev` · **Version:** `0.1.0.dev0` · **Tests:** 16 passing

## Summary

First working version. pyNR now has:

- a Cactus-like flesh (parameter files, thorns, schedule bins, grid functions);
- a uniform 3D grid;
- the ADM evolution system with three slicings;
- analytic initial data for flat-space test waves and for Schwarzschild and Kerr black holes;
- a Gaussian metric perturbation;
- $\Psi_4$ extraction on geodesic spheres;
- Einstein Toolkit (Carpet) output formats, read directly by kuibit.

Every problem in the problem set was run, and its measured result is
recorded in the parameter file header and on its documentation page.

The main scientific finding: plain ADM is good enough to *teach* 3+1 and to
validate the machinery. It is not good enough for clean black-hole physics
(quasi-normal ringdown, long puncture evolutions). That decides the next
milestone: BSSN/Z4c with moving punctures.

## Physics

### What can be simulated

| system | initial data | gauge | status |
|---|---|---|---|
| flat space, gauge wave (AwA) | exact, $H = 1 - A\sin 2\pi(x-t)/d$ | harmonic slicing | ✅ exact solution reproduced, 4th-order convergence |
| linearised plane GW (AwA) | exact TT wave, $A = 10^{-8}$ | geodesic | ✅ amplitude constant to 4 digits over 5 crossings |
| Schwarzschild, isotropic (puncture) | $\gamma_{ij} = \psi^4\delta_{ij}$, $K_{ij} = 0$ | geodesic / 1+log, $\beta = 0$ | ⚠️ fails early (puncture problem, below) |
| Schwarzschild / Kerr, Kerr-Schild | analytic, any spin $\chi$ | exact static lapse and shift | ⚠️ stable for 23–40 $M$, then the ADM instability |
| perturbed Schwarzschild | Kerr-Schild $\times (1 + A e^{-(r-r_0)^2/\sigma^2} Y_{\ell m})$ | exact | ⚠️ extraction works, ringdown hidden |

### Findings

- **Ψ₄ conventions fixed and tested.** For an outgoing wave
  $\Psi_4 = -(E_{ij} - iB_{ij})\bar m^i \bar m^j = \ddot h_+ - i\ddot h_\times$. This is
  the WeylScal4/kuibit convention. The unit test on the linear wave pins the
  sign and the tetrad orientation ($e_\theta = -\hat z$ on the $+x$ axis). The
  spin-weighted harmonics ${}_{-2}Y_{\ell m}$ match the ET Multipole phase
  convention (${}_{-2}Y_{22} = \sqrt{5/64\pi}\,(1+\cos\theta)^2 e^{2i\phi}$).
- **Perturbed black hole (problem 6).** The extracted $\Psi_4$ has exactly
  the symmetry of the perturbation: only $m = 0$, $\operatorname{Im}\Psi_4^{20} \sim 10^{-16}$,
  odd $\ell \sim 10^{-12}$. The burst reaches $r = 6M$ at $t \approx 8$–$10M$.
  However, **no clean quasi-normal ringdown** ($M\omega_{220} = 0.3737 - 0.0890i$,
  period $16.8M$) could be measured. A growing, $\ell = 4$-dominated mode with a
  3–4$M$ period takes over from $t \approx 15M$, before even one QNM period has
  passed. Fits of $e^{-t/\tau}\cos\omega t$ do not return $\omega_{220}$.
  Raising the amplitude to $A = 10^{-2}$ lifts the signal but makes the run
  fail earlier ($t \approx 26M$).
- **Geodesic slicing (problem 3).** The physical expectation is a crash at
  $t = \pi M$, when the throat reaches the singularity. pyNR crashes at
  $t \approx 0.7M$ ($\Delta x = 0.2, 0.1$) and at $0.3M$ ($\Delta x = 0.05$).
  The failure starts at the grid point nearest the puncture. This is not
  the physical singularity: it is the **puncture problem** (see Numerical
  analysis).
- **1+log slicing (problem 4).** The lapse does collapse ($\min\alpha$:
  $0.066 \to 0.023$ by $t = M$). It then overshoots to negative values near the
  puncture, $\max\alpha$ exceeds 1, and the run aborts at $t \approx 4.5M$.
  Slice stretching never gets a chance to develop.
- **Perturbation caveat.** The conformal $(\ell, m)$ perturbation violates
  the Hamiltonian constraint at $\mathcal{O}(A)$. It is a quick way to excite
  a black hole, not physical initial data.

## Numerical analysis

### Discretisation

- 4th-order centred finite differences for all first, second and mixed
  derivatives. The Ricci tensor is computed pointwise from first and second
  metric derivatives. The Numba kernel forms only the two contractions of
  $\partial\Gamma$ that Ricci needs; the NumPy reference forms all of
  $\partial_m\Gamma^k_{ij}$. The two agree to $10^{-9}$ relative for generic data
  and all three slicings, which cross-checks the algebra.
- Kreiss-Oliger dissipation of order 6 ($\mathcal{O}(h^5)$), added after the
  RHS through a MoL hook.
- Method of lines with RK4 (default), SSP-RK3, ICN and RK2; CFL factor 0.25.
- Boundaries: static, Sommerfeld/radiative (2nd-order one-sided
  derivatives), flat and periodic.

### Convergence (automated tests)

| test | quantity | measured order |
|---|---|---|
| Kerr-Schild $\chi = 0, 0.7$, $h = 0.1 \to 0.05$ | $\max|\partial_t U|$ at $t = 0$ | > 3.5 (4 expected) |
| Schwarzschild isotropic, away from the puncture | $\max|\partial_t U|$ | > 3.5 |
| Kerr-Schild $\chi = 0.6$ | $\max(|H|, |M_i|)$ | > 3.5 |
| gauge wave, $\Delta x = 0.04 \to 0.02$, $t = 0.5$ | $\max|\alpha - \alpha_\text{exact}|$ | > 3.5 |
| Lagrange interpolation (order 3) | point values | error < $2\times10^{-5}$ at $h = 0.1$ |
| geodesic quadrature, level 4 (2562 points) | Gram matrix of ${}_{-2}Y_{\ell m}$, $\ell \le 4$ | $|G - I| < 5\times10^{-3}$ |

### Stability findings

1. **Plain ADM is only weakly hyperbolic.** With Kerr-Schild data and the
   exact stationary gauge, a constraint-violating mode grows exponentially
   (e-folding $\approx 7M$) from near the excision boundary. Measured lifetimes
   for Schwarzschild ($M = 1$, domain $\pm 8.25$):

   | $\Delta x$ | excision radius | $\epsilon_\text{KO}$ | fails at |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | 0.5 | 1.2 | 0.2 | 7 $M$ |
   | 0.5 | 1.6 | 0.2 | 26 $M$ |
   | 0.5 | 1.8 | 0.3 | 19 $M$ |
   | 0.25 | 1.6 | 0.2 | 29 $M$ |
   | 0.25 | 1.8 | 0.3 | > 40 $M$ |

   For Kerr $\chi = 0.6$ ($\Delta x = 0.25$, excision $1.6 \approx 0.9\,r_+$,
   $\epsilon = 0.3$) the run fails at 23 $M$. Resolution between the excision
   surface and the horizon, and dissipation, move the failure later. But the
   lifetime does not grow without bound, which confirms a continuum
   (formulation) instability rather than a resolution problem.
2. **Excision bug found and fixed.** The first version zeroed the RHS inside
   the excision region *before* dissipation was added, so excised points were
   not actually frozen. A Kerr-Schild grid point at $r = 0$ also gave NaN
   initial data that dissipation then spread. Fixes: MoL now has a final RHS
   stage that runs after all hooks (excision and boundaries apply there),
   non-finite excised data are replaced by flat values, and the constraints
   are reported as zero inside the excision region.
3. **Puncture problem.** For isotropic data $\psi \sim M/2r$, so the metric
   varies on the scale of the distance to the puncture. At the nearest grid
   point ($r = 0.33M$ for $\Delta x = 0.2$, $\gamma_{xx} \approx 40$) the finite-difference
   Ricci tensor is ~30× too large. Refining only moves a grid point closer
   to $r = 0$, so the failure time does *not* improve. Excision does not help
   with $\alpha = 1$, $\beta = 0$, because the excision surface then has
   incoming characteristics. The standard cures are conformal methods
   (BSSN/Z4c with moving punctures) or horizon-penetrating slices with
   excision.
4. **Plain ADM cannot resolve a QNM ringdown.** The usable window ($\lesssim 20M$
   after the burst) is shorter than the $\ell = 2$ QNM period for
   $M = 1$.

## Implementation

### Architecture (ET mapping)

| Cactus / ET | pyNR |
|---|---|
| flesh, `cctkGH` | `pynr/cactus/`: `Simulation`, `Schedule`, `Parameters`, `GridFunctions`, `UniformGrid` |
| `param.ccl` / `interface.ccl` / `schedule.ccl` | `Thorn.parameters` / `Thorn.setup()` / `Thorn.schedule()` |
| `.par` files | same syntax: `ActiveThorns`, `Thorn::p[i]`, `$parfile`, multi-line strings |
| MoL, Dissipation, NaNChecker | `MoL` (+ RHS hooks, `rhs_final`, blow-up abort), `Dissipation` |
| ADMBase, Exact, ADM | `ADMBase`, `Exact`, `ADMEvolve` |
| WeylScal4, Multipole | `WeylScal4` (on-demand grid functions), `Multipole` (geodesic spheres) |
| CarpetIOHDF5, CarpetIOScalar, IOBasic | `IOHDF5`, `IOScalar`, `IOBasic`: same file names, dataset names and attributes |

Design rules that emerged:

- All 16 ADM variables live in one contiguous `(16, nx, ny, nz)` array. The
  named grid functions are views into it.
- Derived quantities ($H$, $M_i$, $\Psi_4$) are computed lazily, at most once
  per iteration, and only when output needs them.
- Kernels are chosen through `pynr.backends` (`Driver::backend = numba | numpy`),
  so thorns never import a specific implementation.
- Unknown parameters and bad keywords are errors, as in Cactus.

### kuibit compatibility

These were found by reading kuibit's parsers, and the test suite now
checks them:

- Datasets are named `THORN::var it=N tl=0 rl=0 c=0`, with `origin`, `delta`,
  `time` and `cctk_nghostzones`, and data stored as `[z, y, x]`.
- Each file needs the `Parameters and Global Attributes/All Parameters`
  group. pyNR declares `output_ghost_points = no`, because its ghost points
  are ET-style boundary points.
- Scalars are `var.<reduction>.asc`; multipoles are `mp_Psi4_l<l>_m<m>_r<R>.asc`.

### Performance (12-thread Apple M-series, Python 3.14, Numba 0.67)

| step | ADM RHS, single thread | KO dissipation, $96^3$ |
|---|---|---|
| first version | 916 ns/point | 45 ms |
| direction-specialised stencils (`dx, dxy, …`) | 694 ns/point | — |
| contiguous loop order in KO | — | 8.5 ms (5.3×) |
| contracted Ricci + `error_model="numpy"` | 670 ns/point | — |

With 12 threads: 107 ns/point on $96^3$. A full RK4 step of problem 6
($135^3$) takes 1.4 s. The remaining ~3–5× gap to tuned C++ comes from the
per-point scratch arrays: LLVM cannot prove they don't alias, so it neither
keeps them in registers nor vectorises across points.

- **Packaging:** hatchling `pyproject.toml`, `pynr` CLI (`run`, `thorns --markdown`). `python -m build` and `twine check` pass; the name `pynr` is free on PyPI.

## Roadmap ahead / TODO

In priority order. Each item comes with an acceptance test drawn from the problem set.

1. **BSSN (then Z4c) + moving-puncture gauge** (1+log, Gamma-driver shift).
   *Accept:* problem 4 runs for ≥ 500 $M$ with $\min\alpha \to \approx 0.3$;
   problem 6 shows the $\ell = 2$ QNM with $\omega$ within 1 % and $\tau$ within 5 %.
   *Why first:* every physics limitation above traces back to the ADM
   formulation or the puncture problem.
2. **Kernel row-vectorisation along `k`.** *Accept:* ADM/BSSN RHS ≤ 200
   ns/point/thread. Do this together with item 1, so that BSSN is written
   in the fast layout from the start.
3. **Apparent-horizon finder** (fast-flow or spectral): mass, spin, and a
   check of the excision radius against the horizon.
4. **Elliptic solver** (SciPy sparse / PyAMG): constraint-satisfying
   perturbations (Brill waves), Bowen-York / TwoPunctures-type binary data.
   *Accept:* $H$ at $t = 0$ converges to zero for perturbed data.
5. **Fixed mesh refinement** (nested boxes, Berger-Oliger time stepping).
   This is needed to put extraction spheres at $\gtrsim 50M$ at a sensible cost.
6. **Extraction:** extrapolation to $r \to \infty$, strain via kuibit FFI in
   the notebooks, and a Teukolsky-wave test of `Multipole`.
7. **GPU backend** (CuPy or JAX) starting from `adm_numpy`; **MPI** domain
   decomposition with `mpi4py`.
8. **Matter:** a Valencia hydro thorn; TOV-star test.
